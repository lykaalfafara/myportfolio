CPIS — Construction Project Information System

A Construction Project Information System I designed and built in Excel, with a Power BI layer on top, to prove to myself — and now to you — that I could structure a construction project's information, not just enter it.

Excel system — complete Power BI layer — complete

Problem

Construction teams often run on disconnected spreadsheets: one file for documents, another for procurement, another for budget, none of them talking to each other.

Approach

I designed a single Excel workbook where every register — documents, drawings, RFIs, procurement, budget, progress — shares the same structure and can be joined together.

Solution

Seventeen linked registers feed a star-schema data model through Power Query, which a Power BI dashboard reads for reporting.

Impact

It's a working demonstration, not a deployed tool on a live jobsite yet — but it's the clearest proof I have that I can design a system, not just operate one.

About

I'm an electrical engineer who ended up loving the operations side of getting things done.

I studied Electrical Engineering, sat the licensure exam, and became a Licensed Electrical Engineer in the Philippines. Since then, my day-to-day work hasn't been on a jobsite — it's been in customer support, data entry, and now international operations at Cambridge University Press & Assessment.

Somewhere in that work, I noticed what I actually enjoy: taking scattered information and giving it structure. Building the register that replaces five spreadsheets. Automating the email that used to take an hour. Turning a pile of numbers into a dashboard someone can actually use to decide something.

That's why I built CPIS on my own time, and why I'm working through Bluebeam and construction workflows deliberately rather than claiming experience I don't have. I'm not positioning myself as a ten-year construction veteran. I'm positioning myself as an engineer who builds systems — and who's ready to bring that to engineering operations, construction technology, project controls, or digital engineering teams.

If that combination is useful to your team, I'd like to talk.
